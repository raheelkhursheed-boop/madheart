AI policy

silkroute.art does not represent, list, promote or sell AI-generated art. Every work we show is made by a human artist whose hand, judgment and skill are responsible for the finished piece.

Our position

We hold this line for three reasons.

  • Authorship. Curation means vouching for an artist's vision and craft. We cannot vouch for an image a model produced from a prompt.
  • Fairness to artists. Generative models are largely trained on artists' work without consent, credit or payment. Representing their output would compete directly with the people we exist to support.
  • Trust for collectors. Collectors come to us for provenance. A clear human-made guarantee is part of what they buy.

Definitions

AI-generated art is any work where a generative AI system (for example a text-to-image, image-to-image or video model) produced the composition, subject or substantial visual content. This includes work that was prompted, curated, upscaled or lightly edited by a person afterward. It is not accepted.

AI-assisted tools are conventional software features that support a human's own work without generating it. Some are allowed.

UseAllowed?
Text-to-image or image-to-image generation (Midjourney, DALL·E, Stable Diffusion and similar)No
Generative fill, generative expand or AI object insertionNo
AI "style transfer", or training a model on your own work to produce new piecesNo
Using AI output as a base layer, then painting over itNo
Photographing, scanning or digitally painting your own work by handYes
Standard editing: crop, color correction, levels, sharpening, noise reductionYes
Background removal or object selection on your own photo of your own workYes
AI used only for admin tasks (writing a bio, translating a description, scheduling)Yes

When in doubt, the test is simple: if you removed the AI step, would the artwork still exist as you made it? If not, it is AI-generated.

Scope

This policy covers every work shown, listed or sold through silkroute.art, in every medium, physical or digital. It applies to:

  • Artists and estates we represent, and anyone who applies to be shown.
  • Exhibitions, collections, editions and prints, including reproductions of existing works.
  • Images used to promote an artist or a work, such as listing photos, mockups and social posts.
  • Guest curators and partner galleries showing work through us.

Works that explore AI as a subject (for example a hand-painted series about machine vision) are welcome. The rule is about how a work is made, not what it is about.

Artist commitments

Every artist confirms, when they join and with each work, that:

  1. The work is their own and was made by human hands and decisions.
  2. No generative AI produced any part of the work's visual content.
  3. They will disclose any software or digital tools used in the process.
  4. They will keep reasonable evidence of process, such as sketches, work-in-progress photos, layered source files or studio photos, and share it on request.
  5. They will tell us promptly if they later learn a listed work does not meet this policy.

These commitments become part of the artist agreement. Giving false information is a breach of that agreement.

Verification

A curator reviews every artist and every work before it goes live. Detection software alone is not reliable enough to decide, so a person always makes the call.

  1. Artist review. We visit every artist in their studio and look at their portfolio, history and process.
  2. Work review. We see every work in person before it is listed, and may request process evidence for any work.
  3. Spot checks. We re-check a sample of live works regularly, and any work that a buyer, artist or member of the public flags.
  4. Decision. If the evidence shows human authorship, the work goes or stays live. If it does not, the steps below apply.

Violations and appeals

If a work is found or reasonably suspected to be AI-generated, we will act as follows.

SituationWhat happens
Work under reviewHidden from the site while we ask the artist for process evidence.
Undisclosed AI use confirmedWork removed. Any open sale is cancelled and the buyer refunded.
Repeated or deliberate misrepresentationArtist removed and all their works delisted.
AI work already soldBuyer offered a full refund. Costs may be recovered from the artist under the artist agreement.

Artists can appeal any decision by sending new evidence to the curation team. A curator who was not part of the first decision reviews the appeal.

For collectors

Every work bought through silkroute.art comes with a human-made guarantee.

  • Each listing names the artist and the medium and tools used.
  • Collectors can ask for process notes on any work before buying.
  • If a work you bought is later found to be AI-generated, you are entitled to a full refund.
  • You can flag any work you have concerns about, and a curator will look into it.

Our own use of AI

We hold ourselves to the same standard. silkroute.art will never use generative AI to create artwork, alter an artist's work, or produce imagery that could be mistaken for an artist's work.

We may use AI for back-office tasks such as search, translation, fraud detection and customer support. We will say so where it affects you.

We also work to protect artists' images from being used to train AI models:

  • We do not sell or license artists' work for AI training.
  • We block known AI training crawlers from the site.
  • Scraping artwork from silkroute.art for model training is not permitted.

Updates

AI tools change quickly, so we will review this policy at least once a year and post any change with its effective date. The core position will not change: silkroute.art represents human artists only.

Questions, reports or appeals go to the silkroute.art curation team.
